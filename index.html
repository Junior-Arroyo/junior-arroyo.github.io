About

Background & research.

I combine computational analysis with experimental studies to investigate imaging performance and tissue response.

I am a PhD candidate in Biomedical Engineering at Johns Hopkins University, working with Prof. Muyinatu A. Lediju Bell in the PULSE Lab. My training in physics and digital signal and image processing helps me connect how an imaging technology works with what its measurements actually mean.

My experience includes evaluating the safety of emerging imaging techniques, developing automated ultrasound analysis, and studying the properties of biological materials. I have also served as a teaching assistant in physics, signal processing, and medical imaging.

Research in practice

Research areas.

01

Imaging safety & biological differences

How can we acquire useful images while protecting tissue?

Imaging methods that use laser light must balance the information they provide with the risk of tissue injury. My doctoral work combines simulations and in vivo experiments to study that risk across tissues and skin tones.

Published work: injury prediction validated against tissue analysis and measured skin injury thresholds.

Scientific methods

Monte Carlo light transport, finite element heat transfer, tissue damage modeling, in vivo experiments, histology, and immunohistochemistry. Applications include laser safety for photoacoustic imaging and pigmentation-dependent injury thresholds.

Skin tone study ↗Light delivery study ↗Prediction model ↗
02

Imaging for surgical guidance

How can we make important structures easier to distinguish?

I investigate how imaging can help identify structures that are difficult to see during surgery. In a swine study, we used photoacoustic imaging to visualize the ureters—the tubes connecting the kidneys to the bladder.

Published work: a demonstration of ureter imaging in swine using an injected dye to make these structures visible.

Scientific methods

Photoacoustic imaging following intravenous methylene blue administration, with in vivo validation in swine.

Ureter imaging study ↗
03

Image analysis & access to care

How can ultrasound provide useful information where specialists are scarce?

I helped develop and evaluate automated analysis of prenatal ultrasound to estimate fetal measurements and determine fetal presentation and placental location. This research addresses the need for imaging support in communities with limited access to trained specialists.

Published work: evaluation of automated prenatal assessment against expert reference measurements.

Scientific methods

Deep learning, ultrasound image analysis, and evaluation of fetal biometry, fetal presentation, and placental location. Earlier ultrasound work also includes elastography: measuring tissue stiffness from its response to mechanical waves.
